Research Statement:
My research examines the convergence of technology, embodiment, identity, and consciousness through the lens of posthumanism, with a specific focus on cyberpunk and speculative fiction. Through texts such as Richard K. Morgan’s Altered Carbon and William Gibson’s Neuromancer, I study how digital consciousness, artificial embodiment, virtual reality, and technocapitalist systems reshape traditional understandings of the human.
My work is situated at the intersection of English Literature, Digital Humanities, Media Studies, Philosophy of Technology, and Cultural Theory. Drawing on theorists such as Donna Haraway, N. Katherine Hayles, Jean Baudrillard, and other thinkers in posthumanism and cyberculture, I examine how literary narratives imagine new forms of subjectivity in technologically saturated societies. In particular, I am interested in how cyberpunk fiction interrogates the boundaries between body and machine, mind and data, reality and simulation, mortality and technological continuity.
My doctoral research contributes to contemporary literary scholarship by positioning cyberpunk not only as a genre of speculative imagination, but also as a critical site for examining technological power, social inequality, artificial embodiment, and ethical questions surrounding human enhancement. I aim to develop this research further through teaching, publication, interdisciplinary collaboration, and public-facing scholarship on literature, technology, AI, and the future of human identity.
